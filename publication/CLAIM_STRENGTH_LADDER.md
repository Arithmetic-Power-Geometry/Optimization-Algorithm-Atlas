# Claim-Strength Ladder

Claims in the eventual paper must not exceed their evidence level.

## L0 — Descriptive identity
Example: an algorithm uses covariance adaptation.
Required: canonical specification.

## L1 — Source-reported observation
Example: a study reported improved median error on a declared suite.
Required: source + exact conditions.

## L2 — Review synthesis
Example: multiple independent studies consistently report a behavior within a defined regime.
Required: extracted comparable evidence + limitations.

## L3 — Documented boundary/counterexample
Example: formal counterexample or replicated empirical limitation.
Required: primary theoretical source or adequate replicated empirical evidence.

## L4 — Project-validated conditional result
Example: controlled experiments identify a repeatable transition under predeclared conditions.
Required: reproducible raw results, statistical analysis and artifact provenance.

## L5 — General mechanism claim
Example: mechanism M explains a failure across algorithm families.
Required: cross-family evidence, competing explanations tested, ablations and external validation.

## Rule
Never write an L5 sentence from L1/L2 evidence. The abstract and conclusion are audited against this ladder.
