# Final Paper Section Logic — CSR-first

## 1. Introduction — The evidence problem in optimization research
Establish proliferation, fragmentation, benchmark dependence, structural overlap and reproducibility concerns. End with the precise unresolved need: condition-specific evidence synthesis across paradigms.

## 2. Review Questions, Scope and Methodology
Define RQs, databases, search dates/strings, screening, eligibility, extraction, quality control, evidence ontology and reproducibility protocol. Include review-flow figure/table.

## 3. From Algorithm Names to Optimization Mechanisms
Introduce mechanism-first vocabulary and Algorithm DNA. Establish why inspiration labels are insufficient for scientific comparison.

## 4. Historical Evolution and Mechanism Genealogy
Present the Mechanism River and source-traceable ancestry. Distinguish inheritance, adaptation, recombination and genuinely different computational principles.

## 5. Cross-Paradigm Optimization Atlas
Synthesize mathematical/local, gradient/curvature, derivative-free, evolutionary, differential, swarm, probabilistic, surrogate, multiobjective, constrained, robust, dynamic, mixed/bilevel and learning-assisted optimization.

## 6. Structural Similarity and Algorithmic Redundancy
Use normalized fingerprints and sensitivity-tested similarity analysis. Do not equate proximity with plagiarism or lack of value.

## 7. Benchmark Ecosystems and Coverage Bias
Map benchmark suites to problem properties. Identify overrepresented and underrepresented conditions without treating absence as failure.

## 8. Condition-Level Evidence Atlas
Core section. Present DOCUMENTED / PARTIAL / CONTRADICTORY / UNTESTED / PROJECT-VALIDATED cells with confidence.

## 9. Theory, Guarantees and Counterexamples
Separate convergence guarantees, assumptions, complexity, invariances and formal counterexamples from empirical performance.

## 10. Reproducibility and Experimental Credibility
Audit specification, code, parameter disclosure, seeds, benchmark versions, environments and independent reproduction.

## 11. Controlled Failure-Frontier Analysis
Present only project-generated, predeclared experiments. Locate capability transitions rather than produce a universal leaderboard.

## 12. Validated Gaps and Existing Remedies
Apply G0-G5 ladder. For each high-value gap, identify existing remedies and whether controlled evidence resolves it.

## 13. Researcher Decision Framework
Translate the atlas into comparator selection, benchmark selection, novelty checking, application selection and experiment design.

## 14. Research Frontier
Present unresolved high-value evidence cells linked to explicit resolving experiments/theoretical questions.

## 15. Threats to Validity and Limitations
Search coverage, publication bias, implementation effects, benchmark representativeness, temporal cutoff, taxonomy subjectivity and experimental resource limits.

## 16. Conclusions
State only evidence-supported synthesis. No new result should first appear here.

## Narrative arc
fragmentation → mechanism normalization → evidence mapping → credibility audit → controlled validation → actionable frontier.
