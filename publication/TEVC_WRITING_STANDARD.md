# TEVC-Oriented Academic Writing Standard

This standard governs drafting after P031 Evidence Freeze. It describes scholarly conventions; it does not imitate or reproduce the wording of any published article.

## Scientific voice
- concise, technical, neutral and evidence-led;
- mechanism terminology preferred over metaphorical algorithm descriptions;
- distinguish literature-reported evidence from project-validated evidence;
- state the evaluated conditions with empirical claims;
- avoid universal superiority, promotional adjectives and unsupported novelty language;
- separate observation, interpretation and implication.

## Paper logic
1. establish the scientific problem and why existing surveys do not resolve it;
2. state explicit research questions and scope;
3. define review/search/evidence methodology;
4. normalize algorithms into mechanisms and genealogy;
5. synthesize evidence by condition rather than by paper;
6. reconcile supportive and contradictory findings;
7. quantify benchmark/reproducibility coverage;
8. present controlled computational validation only for review-discovered uncertainty;
9. analyze mechanism-conditioned failure boundaries;
10. derive evidence-supported open questions and future research.

## Paragraph discipline
Each substantive paragraph should normally contain:
- proposition;
- supporting source or frozen artifact;
- qualification/conditions;
- synthesis or implication.

## Experimental prose
Report:
- benchmark suite/version and problem IDs;
- implementation/version;
- parameter policy;
- evaluation/time budget;
- seeds/instances;
- failure/timeout policy;
- statistical method and effect magnitude;
- computational overhead where relevant.

Never infer a universal ranking from finite benchmark results.

## Abstract
Write last. It must state problem, methodological distinction, evidence base, principal supported findings and implications without claims stronger than the frozen evidence.

## Introduction
Do not begin as an encyclopedia of optimization. Establish the evidence problem, closest-review boundary and research questions.

## Results and discussion
Organize around research questions and mechanisms, not a sequence of algorithm names. Negative and contradictory evidence is retained.

## Conclusion
No new result or novelty claim may first appear in the conclusion.

## Originality
No published TEVC sentence, paragraph structure, figure or table is copied. Venue conventions and technical rigor are emulated; expression and synthesis are original.
