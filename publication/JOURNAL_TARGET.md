# Journal Target Strategy

## Primary target — Computer Science Review

### Fit
The journal is dedicated to research surveys and expository overviews of open problems in computer science for a broad computer-science readership.

### Atlas framing for this target
The paper must not be a catalogue of nature-inspired algorithms. It should address a general computer-science question:

**What is actually known about optimization mechanisms, under which computational conditions, with what reproducibility, and where do evidence boundaries remain?**

### Differentiation requirement
Computer Science Review already contains recent optimization/metaheuristic reviews, including benchmark-driven functional taxonomy. Therefore the submission must demonstrate additional methodological contribution through:
- cross-paradigm scope;
- atomic condition-level evidence status;
- mechanism ancestry and structural similarity;
- benchmark-property exposure;
- reproducibility status;
- documented counterevidence;
- project-generated failure frontiers;
- explicit resolving experiments;
- machine-readable research atlas.

## Secondary target — Swarm and Evolutionary Computation
Excellent if the final evidence supports narrowing the paper to evolutionary/swarm/metaheuristic mechanisms. The journal explicitly welcomes survey papers and covers constrained, multiobjective, multimodal, dynamic and large-scale optimization.

## Secondary target — Artificial Intelligence Review
Suitable if the final framing emphasizes computational intelligence, evolutionary computation, algorithm selection and AI-oriented optimization.

## Secondary target — Information Sciences
Potentially suitable for a strong theory/practice synthesis with substantial original evidence; surveys are published more selectively.

## Target decision gate
Re-evaluate after evidence freeze. Journal choice must follow the completed contribution rather than forcing results into a predetermined venue.
