\section{Controlled Computational Characterization}
\label{sec:computational}

\subsection{Purpose of the computational study}

The computational component is not intended to identify a universally best optimizer. Its purpose is to demonstrate how the atlas protocol converts a heterogeneous optimization question into condition-indexed, reproducible evidence. Four deliberately different mechanisms are retained: uninformed random sampling, differential population variation, simplex-based derivative-free local search, and covariance-adaptive distribution search. Their behavior is evaluated under the same continuous black-box suite, matched problem conditions, and normalized objective-evaluation budgets.

The resulting experiment should therefore be read as a controlled characterization of the frozen configurations, not as a complete comparison of their broader algorithm families. In particular, no conclusion about all differential-evolution, simplex, covariance-adaptation, or random-search variants follows from one implementation and parameterization.

\subsection{Validated execution scale}

The cross-dimensional publication campaign covers all 24 noiseless BBOB functions at dimensions \(D\in\{5,10,20,40\}\), with evaluation checkpoints at \(100D\), \(300D\), and \(1000D\). The corrected fixed-budget scaling product contains 1,152 dimension--algorithm--function--budget cells. This count is a condition-level summary product and is distinct from the underlying run-level observations.

The EF004 production campaign adds algorithm-separated COCO target-runtime logging. Across its 16 production batches, combined validation accepted 14,400 checkpoint rows and 64 algorithm-labelled COCO result roots. ObjectiveRecorder counts agreed with COCO evaluation counts within the validated batches, and joint official \texttt{cocopp} postprocessing completed successfully. These checks establish the integrity of the measurement pipeline; they do not themselves imply a performance ordering.

\subsection{Two complementary performance views}

The characterization retains fixed-budget and target-runtime views because they answer different questions. Fixed-budget analysis asks how much objective improvement has been obtained after a declared number of evaluations. Target-runtime analysis asks how many evaluations are required to reach a declared target, with unreached targets retained as unsuccessful observations. A method can appear favorable under one view and less favorable under another without contradiction.

For fixed-budget scaling, dimension and FE/D remain explicit axes. For target-runtime analysis, COCO's logger and postprocessing provide the benchmark-authoritative attainment semantics. The project does not reconstruct exact target runtimes from sparse checkpoints and does not infer hidden optimum values from undocumented COCO internals.

\subsection{Discovery and held-out characterization}

The discovery campaign uses BBOB instances 1--5. EF005 repeats the frozen algorithms, dimensions, seeds, and budgets on disjoint instances 6--10. This separation prevents the same benchmark instances from serving simultaneously as the sole source of a discovered pattern and its held-out evaluation.

The held-out campaign is interpreted conservatively. It supports comparison of condition-level target-runtime and scaling behavior across a disjoint instance set. It is not used to create an exact reliability boundary after the results are known. The predeclared frontier protocol required an exact target subset and reliability threshold before confirmation; those numerical choices were not frozen before EF005 execution. Accordingly, no post hoc binary frontier is reported.

\subsection{What the experiment establishes}

Four conclusions about the evidence process are project-validated independently of any algorithm ranking. First, cross-dimensional comparison can be executed with exact objective-call accounting under normalized FE/D budgets. Second, algorithm-separated COCO logging permits authoritative target-runtime postprocessing without conflating comparator identities. Third, discovery and held-out instance sets can be executed under the same frozen scientific configuration. Fourth, a valid experimental campaign can support a weaker characterization claim while failing to justify a stronger frontier claim when the latter's decision threshold was not completely predeclared.

These conclusions matter for optimization benchmarking because reproducibility is not only the ability to rerun code. It also requires that the semantic path from observations to claims be frozen sufficiently early. The EF004 and EF005 campaigns satisfy the execution and provenance requirements for condition-level characterization, while the interpretation audit deliberately limits the strength of the frontier claim.

\subsection{Algorithm-level performance reporting}

Algorithm-level numerical comparisons are reported only through artifacts generated from the promoted EF004 and EF005 data. The official \texttt{cocopp} products provide target-runtime and ECDF-style views, while fixed-budget tables are generated from the validated checkpoint corpus. No values are manually transcribed from plots, and no aggregate rank is used as evidence of universal superiority.

The principal reporting unit is algorithm/configuration \(\times\) function \(\times\) dimension \(\times\) budget, with instance and seed variation retained in the underlying data. Main-text synthesis may group functions by documented BBOB properties only when the property mapping is authoritative and the within-group observations support the aggregation. Function-level products remain available for audit.

\subsection{Cost and fairness boundaries}

Equal objective-evaluation budgets improve comparability but do not equalize computational cost. CMA-ES maintains covariance-related state; differential evolution operates on a population; Nelder--Mead maintains simplex geometry; and Random Search has comparatively little optimizer-side state. Wall-clock or optimizer overhead is therefore interpreted separately from objective-call efficiency.

Likewise, the comparator panel is intentionally small. PSO and GA were not added merely to make the experiment appear broader because their publication implementation and configuration provenance had not passed the same frozen acceptance path when the comparator panel was established. Fewer comparators with inspectable provenance are preferable to a larger panel with weaker specification.

\subsection{No universal ranking}

The experiment does not define an overall winner. Performance depends on function, dimension, budget, target precision, implementation, and configuration. Any descriptive rank or aggregate ECDF is therefore a summary of the evaluated distribution, not an intrinsic ordering of optimization algorithms.

This interpretation is consistent with the mechanism-first atlas. The purpose of empirical comparison is to locate evidence within a condition space and relate observed behavior to mechanisms where justified. It is not to collapse heterogeneous conditions into a single score that erases the circumstances under which the observation was produced.
