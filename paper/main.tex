\begin{abstract}
Optimization research has expanded across mathematical programming, derivative-free search, evolutionary computation, swarm methods, surrogate optimization, multiobjective methods, and learning-assisted optimization, but evidence about these methods is often fragmented across incompatible levels of description. We present a mechanism-first framework that separates algorithm identity, computational structure, theoretical guarantees, empirical evidence, reproducibility, and unresolved questions before synthesis. The study combines a 90-entry cross-paradigm census with 42 source-backed mechanism fingerprints and a condition-level evidence model that distinguishes documented, partial, contradictory, untested, and project-validated states. To connect synthesis with executable evidence, we evaluate Random Search, differential evolution, Nelder--Mead, and CMA-ES on all 24 noiseless COCO BBOB functions across dimensions 5, 10, 20, and 40 under normalized evaluation budgets. Discovery and held-out campaigns produce 14,400 validated checkpoint rows each and algorithm-separated COCO target-runtime outputs. Official ECDF views show substantial condition dependence and no stable universal ordering across targets and problem classes. Because the exact numerical reliability threshold required for a binary failure frontier was not frozen before held-out evaluation, we report characterization rather than retrofit a frontier. The resulting methodology links mechanism, evidence, experiment design, and claim strength while preserving reproducibility and limiting overstatement.
\end{abstract}
