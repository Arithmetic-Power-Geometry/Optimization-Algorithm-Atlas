# Planned Paper Architecture — Written After Evidence Freeze

## Working concept

A foundation-style review should answer not only **what algorithms exist**, but also **what is actually known about them, where the evidence stops, and which experiments are needed next**.

## Recommended article type

Systematic critical review + reproducible computational evidence map.

It is neither:
- a bibliometric-only review;
- a taxonomy-only review;
- a benchmark leaderboard;
- nor a paper built around a preselected new optimizer.

## Proposed structure

1. Introduction
2. Review scope and research questions
3. Search, screening, evidence extraction, and quality-control protocol
4. Evolution of optimization mechanisms
5. Mechanism-first taxonomy
6. Algorithm structural similarity and redundancy
7. Benchmark landscape and benchmark blind spots
8. Comparative evidence by optimization regime
9. Reproducibility and implementation audit
10. Failure-regime and capability-frontier analysis
11. Gap atlas
12. Research-frontier map
13. If justified: gap-derived mechanism/algorithm case study
14. Limitations
15. Research agenda
16. Conclusions

## High-value figures

- optimization mechanism genealogy;
- algorithm-family timeline;
- mechanism similarity network;
- algorithm × problem-property evidence heatmap;
- benchmark-property coverage map;
- reproducibility-status map;
- failure-frontier plots;
- evidence-density timeline;
- gap atlas;
- research-frontier diagram.

## High-value tables

- systematic review protocol;
- representative algorithm registry;
- mechanism/DNA matrix;
- theory/guarantee matrix;
- benchmark-suite comparison;
- fair-comparison protocol;
- reproducibility audit;
- evidence gaps and resolving experiments;
- documented vs experimentally discovered gaps.

## Claim discipline

The paper must distinguish:
- source-reported results;
- review synthesis;
- repository-generated results.

No universal 'best optimizer' claim will be made from finite benchmarks.

## Possible contribution statement

The eventual paper may contribute:
1. an auditable mechanism-first optimization taxonomy;
2. an evidence map connecting algorithms to problem regimes;
3. a structural-similarity analysis separating names from mechanisms;
4. a benchmark-coverage audit;
5. standardized reproducible comparisons;
6. empirical failure-frontier maps;
7. a testable gap registry and research agenda;
8. optionally, a mechanism derived from one validated gap.
